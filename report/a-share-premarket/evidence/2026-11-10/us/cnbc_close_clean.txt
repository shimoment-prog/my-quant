Michael Nagle | Bloomberg | Getty Images Stocks surged on Monday, boosted by a decline in oil prices as well as gains in technology shares after last week's volatility. The Dow Jones Industrial Average popped 1,104.02 points, or 2.15%, to close at 52,389.75. The blue-chip index was supported by gains in stocks such as Goldman Sachs and Caterpillar . The S&P 500 rose 2.35% to end at 7,549.94 for its best day since April 8, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.74% to 25,253.14. With Monday's rally, the Dow is now above the level it closed on Election Day. The S&P 500 and tech-heavy Nasdaq still remain slightly below, however. Oil prices fell on Monday, lifting investor sentiment. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures dropped 4.38% to settle at $86.85 per barrel. International benchmark Brent slid 5.85% to $92.57 per barrel. Friday's market partially rebounded from Thursday's brutal artificial intelligence-driven sell-off, as chip stocks recovered on renewed buying. However, concerns linger in the wake of the Democrats' strong midterm results, with investors fearing that tighter regulations on data center construction, energy use, and development costs could slow the AI infrastructure boom . The tech sector also focused on the bombshell announcement Sunday that Nvidia's board of directors is opening an investigation into co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a businesswoman who U.S. intelligence agencies say is a Chinese spy. Nvidia shares closed down 4%. "Magnificent Seven" names were higher contrary to Nvidia. Amazon gained about 3%, while Tesla and Alphabet climbed 5.5% and 1%, respectively. Microsoft also added almost 3%. Other chip stocks advanced as well, with Broadcom popping nearly 9% and Advanced Micro Devices jumping 13%. The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) — which dropped around 13% last week — rose more than 7%. The recent chip sell-off was "overdone," said Jay Hatfield, CEO at Infrastructure Capital Advisors. The turmoil was still "partially correct," he added, given that there "definitely is a policy overhang from the Democrats in both the short run, but, more importantly, in the long run from '28." To be sure, he believes data centers are ultimately "going to get built" in the U.S. For the broader market, Hatfield said that "it's not like the coast is clear" with Monday's bounce. In fact, he anticipates the S&P 500 will be rangebound through the end of the year. "There are still a lot of overhangs on the market," the CEO said, pointing to elevated oil prices as a result of the Middle East conflict as well as the Federal Reserve's interest rate outlook. This week , investors are looking ahead to key AI-related earnings — CoreWeave , Cisco and Applied Materials are all on deck — as they assess the outlook for tech and whether the recent sell-off has further to run. Stock Chart Icon Stock chart icon S&P 500, 1-day — Nick Wells and Sean Conlon 6 Hours Ago GM’s move to the Nasdaq seen as positive by Citi General Motors' move to the Nasdaq on Nov. 17, and its possible inclusion in the Nasdaq-100 index as early as December, could bring increased investor visibility and focus to the overlooked automaker, according to Citi analyst Michael Ward. If GM joins the Nasdaq-100 index it "would have a 0.2% weighting in the Index which represents about $4.6 billion of buying, or 10% of the non-indexed market cap," he wrote. Additionally, he expects GM to buy back $10 billion in shares by the end of 2027, about 20% of the non-indexed market cap, which will support shares. If it joins, GM will become the cheapest stock in the index with the highest buyback yield, Ward noted. CNBC recently said GM's switch to the Nasdaq could help it reframe its brand identity and boost its depressed valuation with the exposure to a new set of potential investors. Citi agreed. "In our view, the structural improvements at GM and the North American industry have been missed, and if GM becomes part of the Nasdaq 100 Index, new investors will need to take a look which could bring added value to the stock," Ward wrote. The move helps reposition GM as a broad technology-first manufacturer, not just a combustion engine maker. "Consumer connection and complex manufacturing expertise position the auto sector to benefit from developing trends in energy storage, autonomous technologies, and connected platforms," wrote Ward. Citi rates GM a buy with a $131 a share price target, which is more than 50% above Friday's close. — Justin Zacks 7 Hours Ago Stocks making big moves: First Solar, AST SpaceMobile, PDD Holdings An aerial view shows solar panels made by First Solar, during a tour of the Overland Park Solar Array in Toledo, Ohio, U.S., Oct. 5, 2021. The price of the Avalanche token ( AVAX ) jumped 8%, according to CoinGecko. Shares of Avalanche Treasury Corp , which was created to be the proxy trade for the blockchain network, jumped 12% in light trading. Avalanche is a six-year-old blockchain built for business. Unlike many networks aimed primarily at retail traders, Ava Labs, the company that helped develop Avalanche, has for years pursued financial institutions interested in tokenizing real-world assets and moving traditional financial infrastructure onto blockchain rails. Users include BlackRock, Franklin Templeton, Apollo, FIFA and the state of Wyoming. "At the NYSE, we're looking at how blockchain technology can expand what we offer in equities, and we're picking the right technology for each job," said Dennis Lee, head of digital issuance at NYSE, said in a statement.
