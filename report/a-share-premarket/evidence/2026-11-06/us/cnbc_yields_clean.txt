Clary | Afp | Getty Images The 10-year Treasury note yield pulled back on Thursday as investors moved into safe-havens amid a major tech sell-off and looked ahead to the October jobs report due Friday. The benchmark yield was more than 5 basis points lower at 4.839%. The yield on the 2-year Treasury note dropped more than 11 basis points to 4.466%, while the 30-year Treasury declined 2 basis points to trade at 5.26%. One basis point equals 0.01%, or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another. Traders are awaiting Friday's release of the October nonfarm payrolls report at 8:30 am ET. Economists polled by Dow Jones are expecting the report to show a gain of 59,000 on the month. This is set to follow September's weaker-than-expected report , which showed growth of 29,000, with the unemployment rate increasing to 4.2%. The labor market exhibited strength on Thursday, with weekly claims for unemployment benefits falling yet again. Initial filings totaled 189,000 for the week ended Oct. 31 , down 6,000 from the prior week and below the 197,000 Dow Jones forecast. Investors are also still assessing the outcome of the U.S. midterm elections , which saw the Democrats win control of Congress. The so-called blue wave has raised questions around policy for the remainder of President Donald Trump's term, with oil prices falling on expectations that an escalation of the war with Iran has become less likely. Treasury yields moved sharply lower Wednesday in the wake of the Tuesday results, with the 10-year yield ending the session 6 basis points lower. Surging oil prices have put pressure on global government bonds this year, as energy supply constraints in the Middle East raise concerns about a resurgence of inflation and more hawkish central bank policy.
