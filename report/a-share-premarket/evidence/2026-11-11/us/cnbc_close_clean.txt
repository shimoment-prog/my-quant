Angela Weiss | Afp | Getty Images Stocks fell on Tuesday after October inflation data came in hotter than expected and a key Federal Reserve official hinted at the possibility of a half-point rise in rates next month. The S&P 500 lost 0.38% to end at 7,520.93, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 157.86 points, or 0.30%, to 52,231.89. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.74% and closed at 25,065.87. Nvidia dragged down the three major averages, falling more than 5%. The stock sat out Monday's tech-fueled rally following CEO Jensen Huang's admission over the weekend to an affair with an alleged Chinese spy . The consumer price index rose 0.4% in October, above the gain of 0.3% that economists polled by Dow Jones expected. Core CPI — which excludes volatile food and energy costs — increased 0.3% in October, whereas economists had called for a 0.2% advance. Annual figures were not be provided for Tuesday's report because of the 2025 government shutdown. Treasury yields jumped as traders reacted to the report, fearing that interest rate increases could now be more likely in 2027. The 10-year Treasury note yield in particular advanced back above 5% before later moving just below that level. Earlier Tuesday, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in a speech in London that he thinks a December rate hike is likely and opened the door for a possible half-point increase amid persistent inflationary pressures. "While the precise timing of the hikes doesn't matter, ensuring that the promised hikes are implemented in a reasonable period of time does," he said. Traders now see a December rate hike as a near certainty and a small chance of a supersized increase. They also expect at least a quarter-point hike at some point next year. "There's an extrapolation that maybe the Fed's going to have to hike rates more aggressively next year," said Tom Hainlin, national investment strategist at U.S. Bank Asset Management. He later added, "The CPI print said perhaps inflation is going to be a little stickier for a little longer." Tuesday's retreat came one day after the S&P 500 posted its best day since April. Hxdbzxy | Istock | Getty Images Although further interest-rate increases could pressure bond prices in the near term, Wells Fargo strategists believe today's higher yields offer more income and a stronger starting point for long-term returns. The firm examined five-, seven- and 10-year periods and found that the relationship between starting yields and subsequent annualized returns was strongest over approximately seven years. Wells Fargo favors short- and intermediate-term bonds, which offer attractive income without the greater interest-rate sensitivity of longer maturities. High-quality bonds may also provide diversification, reduce portfolio volatility and support cash-flow planning. However, Logan said she thinks two more quarter-point moves would be appropriate before the Fed could take a break. "The exact pace of increases could depend on the near-term economic and financial environment," she said. "After two more increases are in place, it would make sense in my view to pause and examine economic and financial developments to determine what further action may be appropriate.
