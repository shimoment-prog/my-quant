Treasury yields rise as investors weigh midterm vote, jobs data and Iran tensions
Published Tue, Nov 3 2026
4:51 AM EST
Updated 4 Hours Ago
Hugh Leask
Sean Conlon
@SeanAustin96
WATCH LIVE
A trader works as American flags are displayed on the floor of the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) in New York, U.S., on Tuesday, Nov. 29, 2016. Oil slid below $46 as OPEC members failed to bridge differences on production cuts, while a rally in metals fizzled.
Michael Nagle | Bloomberg | Getty Images
Treasury yields moved higher on Tuesday as voting began in the U.S. midterm elections, while investors parse a busy week of economic data and monitored Middle East tensions.
The
10-year U.S. Treasury
yield — the main benchmark for mortgages, auto loans and credit card debt —  rose more than 6 basis points to 4.96%.
The yield on longer-dated
30-year U.S. Treasury
— which tends to move more on geopolitical developments — was up more than 6 basis points at 5.332%.
The
2-year Treasury note
yield, which more closely follows short-term Federal Reserve rate decisions, was up 4 basis points at 4.623%.
One basis point equals 0.01%, or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another.
The moves follow a
mixed day for Treasurys
on Monday, with the benchmark 10-year yield moving 2 basis points lower, and the shorter-dated 2-year note yield rose by more than 1 basis point.
With Americans voting in the
midterm elections
Tuesday, investors are keeping a close eye on how the result will shape Congress and move markets.
On the economic front, September's
JOLTS job openings report
came in better than expected.
ISM services PMI and ADP employment figures are due on Wednesday, with October's nonfarm payrolls and unemployment expected Friday, in a busy week for domestic economic data.
Meanwhile,
oil prices
moved lower as investors grew hopeful about the prospect of a resolution to the Middle East conflict. Oil had initially traded higher following U.S.
strikes
on Iran's Qeshm Island, which heightened concerns of a renewed escalation.
U.S.
West Texas Intermediate futures
for December delivery fell 0.38% to $96.09 per barrel, while
Brent crude
, the global price benchmark, was 1.12% lower at $103.60.
