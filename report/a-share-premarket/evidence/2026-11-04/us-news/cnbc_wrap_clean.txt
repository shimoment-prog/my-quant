S&P 500 snaps 3-day skid on Election Day as chip stocks rebound
Sean Conlon
Deena Zaidi
Lim Hui Jie
Traders work on the floor at the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) in New York City, US, Oct. 26, 2026.
Jeenah Moon | Reuters
The
Nasdaq Composite
rose on Tuesday as investors moved back into key technology stocks after
recent pressure
and geared up for
U.S. midterm election results
.
The tech-heavy index jumped 0.83% and closed at 25,372.01, recouping some of its latest losses. The
Nasdaq-100
rose 0.7% to 28,180.26, remaining in correction territory and down about 10% from its recent high. The
S&P 500
climbed 0.36% to 7,568.92 to break a three-day losing run. The
Dow Jones Industrial Average
lost 197.26 points, or 0.38%, to 51,786.72. The 30-stock index was weighed down by
Apple
shares.
Apple fell almost 2% after the
iPhone maker issued
a disappointing margin forecast late Monday and iPad sales in the fiscal fourth-quarter missed the Street's estimates.
Conversely, shares of chipmakers saw gains.
Advanced Micro Devices
— which releases its third-quarter results after the bell — gained more than 3%, while
Broadcom
rose 3%.
Micron Technology
and
Nvidia
each added more than 2%.
Tuesday's advance marks a reversal from the pullback seen Monday, which sent the Nasdaq down more than 1% and extended a recent retreat in the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 to a third day.
While semiconductor makers — and others tied to artificial intelligence — showed signs of recovery Tuesday, Shawn Snyder of Potomac Fund Management believes the market is not out of the woods.
"The concerns about the cost of capital and the level of debt financing necessary [are] going to persist for quite some time," the economic strategist told CNBC. But the trade that relies on the artificial intelligence buildout could be getting close to oversold territory, he added, saying that "maybe there's a little bit more downside, 4% or 5%, but eventually, if there's not a recession associated with these drawdowns, normally that's kind of where it stops."
Oil prices, meanwhile,
turned lower
on speculation there might be progress in U.S.-Iran negotiations.
Brent crude
lost 1.12% to settle at $103.60 per barrel.
West Texas Intermediate futures
settled 0.38% lower at $96.09 per barrel.
Election Day
Investors are also focused on the
midterm elections
Tuesday, which will decide which party controls the House and Senate starting in January. Prediction market traders see a nearly 75% likelihood of
Democrats taking the Senate
.
A sweep of
the House and Senate by Democrats
could
weigh on the broader market
on Wednesday — and spark a sell-off in artificial intelligence stocks especially — if investors haven't already positioned for that outcome. If Republicans keep control of the Senate or both chambers, the market may see short-term gains, JPMorgan said this week.
Even with President
Donald Trump
in the White House, a victory by Democrats in either the House or Senate could
shape investigations and oversight
in Washington — key for industries including defense, AI and healthcare.
Once the elections are over, it's possible the market may still move higher regardless of the outcome, according to Snyder at Potomac Fund.
"You're looking at an AI trade that's already taken a hit," he said. "That, combined with the idea that the uncertainty from the midterm fades, probably argues for relief rally no matter what."

Starwood's Sternlicht sounds the alarm over neoclouds
Barry Sternlicht, chief executive officer of Starwood Capital, during the Future Investment Initiative (FII) Institute Priority Summit in Miami, Florida, on Thursday, March 30, 2023.
Marco Bello | Bloomberg | Getty Images
Starwood Capital Group CEO Barry Sternlicht thinks some of the smaller, AI-dedicated cloud providers – called neoclouds or neoscalers – are headed for trouble.
"The neo-scalers – maybe some of them will fail," he told CNBC on Tuesday. "Perhaps somebody will buy CoreWeave."
Neocloud
CoreWeave
has been teetering on the edge in recent weeks, due in part to the massive amount of debt that it carries on its balance sheet.
Sternlicht's assessment echoes an October analysis from Gartner that predicted that 80% of neoclouds could cease independent operations by 2030.
Gartner found last month that "enterprise and consumer AI revenue will be insufficient to support profitable returns on all planned AI capital expenditures" between 2027 and 2030.
— Tobias Burns

A blue wave in the midterms could rattle the market
Traffic lights are visible near the US Capitol is seen at sunrise in Washington, DC, November 3, 2026.

Democratic success in midterms could send AI regulation jitters through markets, analyst says
Advocates urge Hochul to slow down, expand the review process, and allow the public to meaningfully engage as the Public Service Commission meets as part of the review process for a data center moratorium on Wednesday, Oct. 28 2026, at the Empire State Plaza in Albany, N.Y.
Lori Van Buren | Albany Times Union | Getty Images
A strong showing from Democrats in Tuesday's midterm elections could go hand-in-hand with financial market jitters stemming from concerns about the regulation of AI, according to one top fixed income analyst.
"The better the Democrats perform, the more nervous the market is likely to become regarding AI regulations," Ian Lyngen, head of U.S. rates strategy at BMO Capital Markets, said in Tuesday note to clients.
Lyngen is expecting gridlock to result from the midterms – a situation in which one party controls one or both chambers of Congress, with the other party controlling the White House. This usually means that no large, programmatic policy changes can make it through the legal process, though smaller agreements and compromises usually can.
"Even with this backdrop, and the price action of the last week, the equity market is holding up relatively well – all things considered," Lyngen said.
Other analysts and commentators have made the opposite observation from Lyngen, saying that gridlock could actually be good for some technology companies, since it may help to preserve a legislative and regulatory status quo.
Such conditions may be advantageous to frontier algorithm companies that are seeking competitive protections within the rapidly commodifying field of artificial intelligence.
While Democratic success at the state and local level may pose obstacles for the physical data center buildout, it may be a good thing for industry at the federal level, Rob Rains at Washington Analysis told CNBC.
"At the federal level, I would say that efforts to try to protect industry and to try to fence off [or] undercut Chinese competitors are likely to pick up considerably because that will be a huge priority for a Democratic Congress or a Democratic Senate," Rains said.
He added: "It will put the President and the White House in a difficult spot because I think, given their druthers, they would rather not confront those issues at this time."
— Tobias Burns

Bayer posts beat-and-raise quarter as pharma outperforms
Bill Anderson, CEO of Germany's drug maker Bayer AG, poses for a portrait session next to the company's logo at the Bayer headquarters in Leverkusen, Germany, December 10, 2024.
Wolfgang Rattay | Reuters
