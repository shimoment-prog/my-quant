Brendan McDermid | Reuters The Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 fell on Monday, giving up earlier gains, as investors continued to move out of tech stocks and add exposure to other parts of the market. The tech-heavy Nasdaq dipped 1.07% to end at 25,164.32, while the S&P 500 shed 0.51% to close at 7,541.40. Both benchmarks were up as much as 1.4% and 1.1%, respectively. The Dow Jones Industrial Average outperformed, rising 213.67 points, or 0.41%, to 51,983.98. Shares of Nvidia and Meta Platforms each dipped 1%. Apple , which reports earnings after the bell, closed nearly 2% lower. Marvell Technology shares dropped almost 13%, while Lumentum shed 18% after the companies announced that Marvell is buying Lumentum in a $115 billion deal . Oracle shares also declined 8% after the company said it plans to sell $10 billion in common stock as well as $10 billion in bonds to finance its AI buildout. Gains in so-called old economy stocks such as Sherwin-Williams and Home Depot powered the Dow, rising almost 6% and 3.6%, respectively. Honeywell International, 3M and Boeing were also up more than 2% each. Stock Chart Icon Stock chart icon Nasdaq intraday chart The rotation out of tech after the major U.S. stock benchmarks suffered a losing week, as investors continued to scrutinize names related to the AI buildout. "The stock market's longer-term bullish narrative may have taken some hits in recent weeks, but it's still intact," said Chris Larkin, managing director of trading and investing at E-Trade. "The challenge has been short-term momentum — stringing together more than a few days without being disrupted by surging oil prices, concerns about AI spending, inflation, or geopolitics." Last week, the tech-heavy Nasdaq and the S&P 500 logged a losing October amid AI spending concerns, with the Nasdaq suffering its worst losses since March 2025. The Dow, however, rose almost 2% as investors rotated into defensive areas like healthcare. "The market has more potential 'volatility triggers' to face — including this week's midterms and jobs data — but its ability to bend but not break under pressure has been a positive sign," Larkin added. The U.S. midterm elections will be held on Tuesday, when Democrats are expected to take control of the House of Representatives, while Senate control remains uncertain. Meanwhile, Brent crude tumbled 7.63% to close at $104.77 per barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures also pulled back 7.36% to settle at $96.46 per barrel. Oil prices fell amid hopes that the U.S. and Iran may resume peace talks, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying over the weekend that President Donald Trump "hasn't made a decision" to attack Iran and that Washington would prefer to resolve the conflict "peacefully." Iranian state media also reported that the country's foreign minister said an agreement with is possible if the U.S. "demonstrates goodwill."
