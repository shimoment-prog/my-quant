Brendan McDermid | Reuters Stock futures were little changed on Monday night, following a losing session as Nvidia once again dragged down the tech sector. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures inched up 8 points, or 0.02%. S&P 500 futures were up 0.06%, while Nasdaq-100 futures advanced 0.1% Asia-Pacific markets opened lower Tuesday. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.76%, while the Topix declined 1.06%. South Korea's Kospi dropped 1.57%, while the small-cap Kosdaq lost 0.62%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 declined 1.37%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index fell 1.17%, while mainland China's CSI 300 opened flat. In regular U.S. trading, Nvidia shares pulled back 4.1%, as traders dumped the chipmaker ahead of its fiscal third-quarter report, due Tuesday after the bell. Other stocks linked to the artificial intelligence trade fell as well, including AMD, Micron Technology and Sandisk. "The tape feels increasingly fragile as Nvidia's AI premium continues to unwind and investors reassess the durability of the ecosystem built around its dominance," said Ben Emons, founder of FedWatch Advisors, in a note. "This backdrop has fueled the 'dispersion trade,' gradually eroding the extreme concentration that has defined the S&P 500 and Nasdaq." Nvidia has been tumbling since last week. Jensen Huang resigned from the CEO post Thursday after reports emerged that he had multiple affairs , including one with an alleged Chinese spy . Further, the chipmaker's board of directors is not considering bringing Huang back as CEO, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC. The stock has dropped almost 24% this month, putting it on pace for its biggest monthly decline since April 2022 — when it plunged 32%. The 30-stock Dow on Monday slid more than 700 points, or 1.4%, while the S&P 500 lost 1.9%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.5%, its biggest one-day decline since Nov. 5. On Tuesday, investors will pore through a slew of economic data, including October retail sales and import prices.
