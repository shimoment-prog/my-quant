Michael M. Santiago | Getty Images U.S. equities fell on Monday, dragged down by key technology stocks, as traders reacted to more developments surrounding Nvidia . The Nasdaq Composite declined 2.49% to end at 24,509.76, while the S&P 500 dropped 1.92% to 7,416.59. Both indexes slid for the third straight session. The Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 739.38 points, or 1.41%, closing at 51,608.17. Shares of Nvidia were down 4.1%. The pressure came as a source familiar with the matter told CNBC that the chipmaker's board of directors is not discussing bringing back Jensen Huang as its chief executive. Adding to the pressure, the Commerce Department on Monday moved to close a loophole that has let Chinese firms rent Nvidia's computing power located in other countries. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent over the weekend also called for a " dramatic shakeup " at the company, urging its board to look outside the company for its next CEO. "Two weeks ago investing in this name looked like a safe journey down a well-lit long hallway," said Mike Mussio, chief investment officer at FBB Capital Partners. Now, he added, "there are several dark corners that people feel they need to peek around to glean additional information before moving forward." "There is plenty to digest, and many jumpy investors seem unwilling to stick around for answers," Mussio continued. The embattled chipmaker is preparing to report its third-quarter results after the bell on Tuesday. While analysts expect the company to post nearly 100% year-over-year earnings and revenue growth, investors will be more focused on how the company will move forward after Huang's exit as CEO. Huang last week resigned from his post after reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. Nvidia shares dropped about 13%, marking their biggest weekly slide since April 2025. "Strong results won't resolve the questions surrounding Jensen's departure," said Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets. "For a company that spent 33 years under one CEO, this is about more than beating expectations. Nvidia now needs to show that the business can continue delivering without the man who built it." Other stocks linked to the artificial intelligence trade were under pressure along with Nvidia. Fellow chipmakers Micron Technology and Intel lost more than 7%, while Advanced Micro Devices declined 5%. Memory chip name Sandisk pulled back about 8%. Additionally, shares of computer maker Dell Technologies also declined almost 7%, while software stock Oracle moved down about 5%. The sell-off broadened beyond just AI-linked names as the session went on, with selling pressure picking up in areas like financials. In that group, Morgan Stanley , Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase shed more than 2%. Another key test for the trade this week is Anthropic's initial public offering .
