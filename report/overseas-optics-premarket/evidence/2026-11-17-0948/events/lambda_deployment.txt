                <div class="blog-post-single__body">
                    <div class="richtext">
                        <span id="hs_cos_wrapper_post_body" class="hs_cos_wrapper hs_cos_wrapper_meta_field hs_cos_wrapper_type_rich_text" style="" data-hs-cos-general-type="meta_field" data-hs-cos-type="rich_text"><p style="text-align: center;"><em><span>Deployment connects 10,000+ NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs&nbsp;and demonstrates lower latency, cooler optics, and improved signal integrity with co-packaged optics networking</span></em></p>
<p><strong><span>SAN FRANCISCO, CA, November 16, 2026</span></strong><span> <span style="color: #0b0b0b; background-color: #e7e6d9;">—</span>&nbsp;</span><a href="/" rel="noopener"><u><span>Lambda</span></u></a><span>, the Superintelligence Cloud, today announced the world’s first large-scale deployment of co-packaged optics (CPO) networking, connecting 10,000+ NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs using NVIDIA Photonics networking switches at its Quebec campus. Following its early adoption of NVIDIA CPO networking, first </span><a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251120689633/en/Lambda-Leads-Early-Adoption-of-NVIDIA-Co-Packaged-Optics-for-Next-Gen-AI-Factories" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><u><span>announced</span></u></a><span> in November 2025, Lambda benchmarked the technology against conventional optics, demonstrating lower latency and improved signal stability at scale. </span></p>
<p><span>As AI clusters grow, networking infrastructure becomes increasingly critical to connecting thousands of GPUs efficiently. Unlike conventional pluggable optics, which convert electrical signals to light at the switch’s front panel, CPO places optical engines alongside the switch ASIC, shortening the electrical path from inches to millimeters. This reduces signal loss, eliminates the need for retimer chips, and increases network resiliency.</span></p>
<blockquote>
<p><span>“Networking has to keep pace with the demands of increasingly large AI clusters. Bringing co-packaged optics into a deployment of this scale is an important step toward building the&nbsp;efficient and resilient infrastructure needed to support the next generation of AI,” said </span><span style="font-weight: bold;">Dave Ward, President of Cloud Services at Lambda</span>.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The deployment uses NVIDIA Photonics switches, featuring 144 800Gb/s ports, liquid cooling, and 18 removable external light sources.<strong> In benchmarks across rack, scalable unit, and pod configurations, CPO maintained identical throughput and more stable signal integrity than conventional pluggable optics, while delivering lower latency at every scale, with the gap growing from 3.8 percent at rack to 10.1 percent at pod.</strong><span style="font-weight: normal;"> </span>The CPO optics also operated at a lower median temperature of 36°C, compared with 51°C for conventional pluggable optics. Cooler, more stable optics drive up to 15x higher reliability to help keep large training jobs running.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“In an AI factory, the network is a business accelerator. Every interruption stalls the job, and every watt counts. NVIDIA Photonics cuts network power, improves resiliency and delivers up to 15x higher reliability, so Lambda’s customers get more productive GPU hours from their investment,<span>”</span>&nbsp;said <span style="font-weight: bold;">Gilad Shainer, Senior Vice President Networking at NVIDIA</span>.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3><strong>Pairing power-efficient networking with an energy-efficient campus</strong></h3>
<p>Located in Quebec, the campus operates with 142 MW of secured, nearly 100% renewable energy capacity, enables free cooling up to 80% of the year, and is guaranteed to a PUE of 1.2, with no process water consumption associated with server cooling.</p>
<p>The deployment will be featured in NVIDIA’s SC26 special address, offering a closer look at Lambda’s implementation of CPO networking at scale. <a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/lambda-photonics" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Read more on the NVIDIA blog</a>.<a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/lambda-photonics" rel="noopener" target="_blank"></a></p>
