Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors 
AGENCY: The Department of Commerce. 
ACTION: Interim final rule. 
SUMMARY: In this interim final rule (IFR), the Department of Commerce is establishing new 
rules to prevent certain foreign actors from leveraging advanced computing power based on U.S. 
technology to threaten U.S. national security and foreign policy interests. 
First, this rule establishes new restrictions which will apply for exports, reexports, or 
transfers (in-country) to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of 
compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau. These restrictions will 
undermine the ability of companies headquartered in countries of concern from using U.S. 
advanced Super Intelligence (SI) semiconductors to train advanced SI models. Data centers, 
foreign foundries, and chip exporters are required to perform robust due diligence on their 
customers, including any direct or indirect ownership or control by persons in Country Group 
D:5 or Macau, as part of complying with this new control. The license requirement will not apply 
to commercial recommender systems, such as those used by online retailers or social media 
companies, as these functions do not pose the same national security threat.  
Second, in addition to the current license requirements for advanced semiconductors, a 
license will be required to export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) advanced semiconductors to 
PAGE 2
2 
 
Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil (and any additional country designated by 
the Secretary of Commerce, in consultation with the Secretary of State or the Assistant to the 
President for National Security Affairs) – countries which present a higher-than-normal risk of 
diversion. The license requirement for these five countries will not apply to semiconductors 
destined to companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, 
the United States, provided certain reporting requirements are met. Commerce will be actively 
monitoring for countries that tolerate evasion of this IFR and will consider a range of 
consequences for such countries, such as import restrictions, new license requirements on 
advanced semiconductor exports, downgrading the country’s status in the U.S. export control 
regime, and Entity List designations and temporary export prohibitions for foreign persons 
involved in such activities. 
Third, this IFR amends the presumptions which front-end chips fabricators and chip 
packagers face when exporting advanced-node logic chips. These amendments reduce the 
number of pathways to overcome the presumptions for chip designers located in destinations 
which face a license requirement for SI chips. This change gives the U.S. government critical 
oversight over companies headquartered in certain countries that seek to leverage advanced 
semiconductor production capabilities in third countries. 
Finally, the IFR formally rescinds the regulations implementing the Biden-era AI 
Diffusion Rule. The AI Diffusion Rule has never been enforced. The regulatory changes in this 
rule formalize the rescission of the AI Diffusion Rule, which the Department of Commerce 
Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) announced in May 2025.

I. Background 
In this IFR, BIS is amending the EAR (15 CFR parts 730-774) to establish new controls 
to ensure that computing power, based on U.S. technology located in foreign data centers, is not 
used in a manner contrary to U.S. national security or foreign policy interests. This IFR 
accomplishes this by:  
• Adding an end-use control requiring a license for exports, reexports, or transfers (in-
country) of advanced SI1 items when there is “knowledge” the items are destined for use 
in data centers, wherever located, that will provide direct or indirect access to a 
significant volume of computing power to or for an entity headquartered in, or whose 
ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or destination specified in Country 
Group D:5. License applications will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis. These changes 
are contained in new § 744.24(a)(1). In addition, if subsequent to the original export of

directly or indirectly, significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered, or

obtain a license. Any data center providing significant volume of compute power to

future export restrictions. However, the license requirement will not apply to ‘commercial 
recommender systems,’ such as those used by online retailers and social media

 This new control is a logical extension of existing controls. At present, a license is 
required to export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) advanced SI semiconductors for 
entities headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Country 
Group D:5 or Macau. Through this rule, BIS is imposing restrictions that will undermine 
those entities’ ability to access significant volumes of advanced SI semiconductors

• Adding a destination-based license requirement for advanced SI items for destinations for

By making these changes, this IFR prevents the use of certain SI items in data centers and 
at a computing power level that can be used for training or running of advanced SI models. 
Access to such computing power can be used to train and run advanced SI models capable of 
supporting military- and intelligence-related activities that could threaten U.S. national security 
or foreign policy interests, e.g., using advanced SI for developing more effective weapons of 
mass destruction and advanced conventional weapons, coordinated cyber-espionage and targeted 
cyber-disruption, algorithmic theft, and weaponized disinformation.  
Specifically, BIS is enhancing and streamlining the licensing policy on items controlled 
under Export Control Classifications Numbers (ECCNs) 3A090 and 4A090 and the 
corresponding .z items by using a combination of more focused and effective destination-based 
controls and enhanced end-use controls. This IFR amends the EAR to reflect a revision to the 
license review policy for these items in parts 742 (Regional stability) and 744 (Control Policy: 
End-User and End-Use Based) to simplify the licensing process for SI integrated circuits (ICs) 
and other SI-related items. The changes promulgated in this IFR follow extensive U.S. 
government consideration of the impact of these controls on U.S. national security and foreign 
policy interests.

III. End-Use Restrictions 
A. Changes to part 744—Control Policy: End-User and End-Use Based 
This IFR adds new § 744.24 Restrictions on exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) 
of certain advanced computing items for use in data centers for end-uses and destinations of 
concern. Section 744.24 provides, in addition to the license requirements set forth elsewhere in 
the EAR, a prohibition on exports, reexports, or transfers (in-county) of advanced SI items 
without a license if, at the time of export, reexport, or transfer (in-country), you have 
“knowledge” that the item will be or is intended to be used in a data center end-use that is 
prohibited under paragraphs (a)(1) of § 744.24.  
You may not knowingly export, reexport, or transfer (in-county) items specified in 
paragraph (a) of § 744.24 for use in a data center to or within any destination worldwide that will 
provide access to more than 12,000,000 “aggregate TPP” (either at one data center or through 
‘linked data centers’ (including digitally-linked infrastructure, not just physically-linked 
infrastructure) to companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is 
headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5 in supplement no. 1 to 
part 740 of the EAR.  ‘Linked data centers’ are data centers that use networking infrastructure 
that links two or more separate data centers together to allow physical and virtual facilities to 
share resources, balance workloads, and replicate data as a unified whole. This prohibition is 
described under § 744.24(a)(1) and the definition is provided in § 744.24(d). The term “for an 
entity” as used in this § 744.24(a)(1) applies to instances in which there is “knowledge” that a 
data center is providing remote access to a non-D:5 customer that is in turn training SI models

for, or participating in the training of SI models for, or providing SI models to a D:5 company. 
This control will cut off additional potential diversion pathways and evasion efforts. 
These controls are necessary because providing computer power access at this level or 
greater will allow for training or running of advanced SI models;  the Secretary of Commerce has 
determined that it could pose a national security and foreign policy threat to the United States 
when such computing power access will be provided to or for companies headquartered, or 
whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country 
Group D:5 – unless the computing power is exclusively used for commercial recommender 
systems. Access to advanced computing power presents significant national security and foreign 
policy concerns, primarily by enabling foreign countries or other entities of concern to accelerate 
the development of weapons of mass destruction (WMD), execute sophisticated zero-day 
cyberattacks on critical infrastructure, and disrupt global military balances of power – unless the 
computing power is exclusively used for commercial recommender systems.  
Consistent with the national security and foreign policy concerns outlined in this IFR, 
BIS is imposing a notification and authorization requirement for subsequent changes in end-use. 
This requirement, provided in new paragraph (e) of § 744.24, and described in more detail 
further in this section, is triggered if there is a change in end-use of the items described in 
paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24. In the event of such a change, a BIS authorization may be required.

IV . Rescinded Provisions of the AI Diffusion Rule 
A. Revoking model weights controls 
This IFR revokes the controls on model weights published in the AI Diffusion Rule. BIS 
has determined that a more effective approach to address national security and foreign policy

concerns that arise from the transfer of model weights is through the use of specified destination-

D. Foreign-Direct Product Rules 
BIS is retaining the worldwide destination scope of the advanced computing foreign 
direct product (FDP) rule in § 734.9(h)(2)(i) if there is “knowledge” that a foreign-produced item 
is destined worldwide or will be incorporated into any “part,” “component,” “computer,” or 
“equipment” not designated EAR99 to any location worldwide. Retention of this provision 
ensures the prohibitions of relevant controls are not otherwise limited.  
Though BIS is not making any changes to § 734.9(h), BIS notes that the agency has 
received questions about the application of that paragraph. Consistent with the robust controls 
implemented in this IFR, BIS notes that provision’s expansive application. If a foreign-produced 
product is subject to the EAR pursuant to § 734.9(h), BIS retains jurisdiction of that item after its 
incorporation into another foreign-produced item, as provided by the text of § 734.9(h)(2)(i). For 
example, if an ECCN 3A090 IC is incorporated into a foreign-produced “part,” “component,” 
PAGE 17
17 
 
“computer,” or “equipment” (such as a GPU board or server), BIS continues to have jurisdiction 
over the IC and license requirements may apply to the export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) 
of the IC (and by extension, the server). Generally, BIS does not retain jurisdiction over items 
after incorporation into other items, absent the applicability of the EAR’s de minimis provisions. 
But in this context, specifically, in the relevant ECCN entries (ECCN 4A090 and those ECCNs 
with a .z paragraph), the CCL notes that such items only meet the specifications for those 
ECCNs if they contain an ECCN 3A090 IC. Thus, even third parties who only receive a 4A090 
item are on notice that such items contain an ECCN 3A090 IC. Furthermore, the retention of 
BIS’s jurisdiction under § 734.9(h) after incorporation is reflected by past BIS guidance to 
inform parties in the supply chain of the applicability of the FDP rule, including the Model 
Certification for Purposes of the FDP Rule of supplement no. 1 to part 734 of the EAR. 
In addition, consistent with the removal of ECCN 4E091, this IFR removes the AI Model 
weights FDP rule in § 734.9(l). 
E. License Exception Advanced Compute Manufacturing ACM 
 BIS is retaining License Exception ACM under § 740.28 due to the benefits this license

VI. Conforming Changes 
In addition to the changes described in Sections II and III, this IFR makes conforming 
changes to the EAR due to the changes described in this preamble. 
A. Revisions to part 743  
 For a transaction to be excluded from the license requirements of § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(1), the 
exporter, reexporter, or transferor must comply with the reporting requirement introduced by this 
IFR in new § 743.10 Reporting Requirements to Qualify for Exclusion from Certain Regional 
Stability Requirements. The reporting requirement mitigates the risk that transactions excluded 
from this license requirement will be used or diverted contrary to the EAR by allowing the U.S. 
Government to monitor the items being exported, reexported, or transferred (in-country).  
 New § 743.10 describes in paragraph (b) the information to be included in the report; in 
paragraph (c), where to submit the report; in paragraph (d), the timeframe as to when reporting is 
required; and in paragraph (e), contacts for general information or questions about this report.  
B. Addition to part 772 
 In addition to removing the definition for “model weights” described in section IV .A. of 
this preamble, this IFR adds a definition to §772.1 for “Aggregate TPP” to describe the sum TPP 
of a group of ICs or commodities.

Technical Note 2 to 3A090.a and 3A090.b:  
1. ‘Total processing performance’ (‘TPP’) is 2 × ‘MacTOPS’ × ‘bit length of the operation’, 
aggregated over all processing units on the integrated circuit. 
a. For purposes of 3A090, ‘MacTOPS’ is the theoretical peak number of Tera (1012) operations 
per second for multiply-accumulate computation (D = A × B + C). 
b. The 2 in the ‘TPP’ formula is based on industry convention of counting one multiply-
accumulate computation, D = A × B + C, as 2 operations for purpose of datasheets. Therefore, 2 
× MacTOPS may correspond to the reported TOPS or FLOPS on a datasheet. 
c. For purposes of 3A090, ‘bit length of the operation’ for a multiply-accumulate computation is 
the largest bit-length of the inputs to the multiply operation. 
PAGE 82
82 
 
d. Aggregate the TPPs for each processing unit on the integrated circuit to arrive at a total. 
‘TPP’ = TPP1 + TPP2 + . . . . + TPPn (where n is the number or processing units on the 
integrated circuit). 
2. The rate of ‘MacTOPS’ is to be calculated at its maximum value theoretically possible. The 
rate of ‘MacTOPS’ is assumed to be the highest value the manufacturer claims in a manual or 
brochure for the integrated circuit. For example, the ‘TPP’ threshold of 4800 can be met with 
600 tera integer operations (or 2 × 300 ‘MacTOPS’) at 8 bits or 300 tera FLOPS (or 2 × 150 
‘MacTOPS’) at 16 bits. If the integrated circuit is designed for MAC computation with multiple 
bit lengths that achieve different ‘TPP’ values, the highest ‘TPP’ value should be evaluated 
against parameters in 3A090. 
3. For integrated circuits specified by 3A090 that provide processing of both sparse and dense 
matrices, the ‘TPP’ values are the values for processing of dense matrices (e.g., without 
sparsity). 
4. ‘Performance density’ is ‘TPP’ divided by ‘applicable die area’. For purposes of 3A090, 
‘applicable die area’ is measured in millimeters squared and includes all die area of logic dies 
manufactured with a process node that uses a non-planar transistor architecture.

gigabytes per second per square millimeter.
