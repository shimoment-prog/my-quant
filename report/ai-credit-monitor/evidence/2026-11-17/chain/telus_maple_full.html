Telus backs out of Vancouver AI data centre projects

Published 2:30 pm Monday, November 16, 2026

By Mark Page

Telus is backing out of a partnership to build two Vancouver data centres. ((Contributed))
Getting your Trinity Audio player ready...

Telus is backing out of a partnership to build two data centres in Vancouver.

“Telus has decided not to proceed with co-developing two previously announced Vancouver data centres with Westbank,” a company spokesperson said via email.

Westbank says it will continue to pursue the projects.

“We are proceeding with the projects as planned, with a new partnership that will be announced in the coming months,” a Westbank spokesperson said.

The data centres are planned for the Mount Pleasant and downtown Vancouver neighbourhoods, and will draw 150 megawatts. The excess heat generated from the centres is to be used to heat homes.

Ottawa is supporting the projects through a program aimed at creating sovereign data centre infrastructure.

And BC Hydro is making a $90-million upgrade to the local Mount Pleasant electrical substation to provide more power to the area’s residential and commercial customers.

While it is no longer pursuing this project, a Telus spokesperson said the company remains “aligned” with the federal sovereign data centre initiative.

Telus is actively expanding a facility in Kamloops and “evaluating new development partners and strategic locations to support the long-term expansion of our sovereign compute capabilities in B.C.”

B.C. Green Party Leader Emily Lowan celebrated the Telus decision, crediting the No AI Vancouver protest campaign.

“From day one, Vancouverites, British Columbians, and the B.C. Greens have opposed these AI data centres because they’ve been pushed through with no public accountability, and because we see them for what they are: another way for big tech to plunder our public assets and drive up costs for working people,” she said in a news release.

The B.C. Green Party wants a moratorium on new data centres in B.C.

Currently, the only restrictions relate to the amount of power BC Hydro can supply to all new data centre projects each year combined, limiting the power supply to 200 megawatts per year for two years, starting in 2026.

Companies will have to bid for the right to use some of that power, but projects already underway when those rules were created, such as this one in Vancouver, are free to connect.
