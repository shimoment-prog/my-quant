Commission File No.: 001-36861 On November 2, 2026, Matt Murphy, CEO of Marvell Technology, Inc. (“Marvell”), and Michael Hurlston, CEO of Lumentum Holdings Inc. (“Lumentum”), participated in an interview on “CNBC; Squawk on the Street.” A copy of the transcript for the interview can be found below. Matt Murphy and Michael Hurlston CNBC; Squawk on the Street Interview Jim Cramer: Let’s get to the deal of the morning. Some would say the deal of the year. That’s Marvell buying Lumentum. And both CEOs join us now. First on CNBC: Matt Murphy and Michael Hurlston. Matt, I’m going to start with you because you are the acquiring company. Please tell us why. What is the rationale behind this deal? Matt Murphy: Hey, good morning, Jim, and great to see everybody. Yeah, this is a really interesting time in the AI data center semiconductor world. This combination takes Marvell, who’s a leader in semiconductor technology for AI connectivity. Michael runs Lumentum, which is the leading company in optical technology for AI data center connectivity. We’re putting the two together to create the category leader in AI connectivity. Jim, this is a trend that’s been happening for some time where optics is moving inside the data center in a more meaningful way. And now in the scale-up world where you want to connect hundreds and thousands of GPUs together in compute systems, you need scale-up technology that needs semiconductors and optical working seamlessly together. And we are going to create a new category of company here. Jim Cramer: Okay. And so Michael, let me go to you. We had you on recently and I was thinking, your company is on fire, Matt’s is too. How are the numbers and why would you want to sell with the kind of trajectory that you have created? Michael Hurlston: Hey, Jim, good to see you. You know, first, the numbers. We just reported our quarter, and it was probably some of the best numbers that we’ve put up. Incredible growth, $350 million of incremental growth on the quarter, driving our gross margins down to 53%, which is the best we’ve ever seen. So our numbers are super strong. Why now? Well, we think that the time is right. Matt talked about a previous acquisition that he did with Inphi, where he saw around the corners and figured out that there was a trend around optical. And we think that the similar trend—seeing around the corner, the similar trend is here—where we have a convergence between the semiconductor world and the optical world. And if we don’t, if we aren’t the first mover, we’re going to be left behind. So we’re moving quickly, acting decisively, and really trying to catalyze this new category that Matt talked about. David Faber: Matt, it’s David. You know, speaking to a couple of your shareholders this morning, they mentioned to me this question they have, and obviously, I’d love to hear you answer it, which is: do you view Lumentum’s key products as sort of differentiated technology, or is there a concern at least they seem to have that it’s really just a commodity that’s in short supply, and that it may not be in short supply by the time you actually get this deal done, which could be over 18 months from now? Matt Murphy: Right. Hey, David. Thank you. Yeah, we’ve heard that narrative and I think it’s a great opportunity to correct the narrative. So first of all, this indium phosphide and laser technology that Lumentum produces is highly differentiated. And while there’s a discussion of a supply shortage today, there’s a supply shortage across the entire global semiconductor and optical industry. The lasers is one of those. The reality is these light sources and lasers couple directly with our proprietary semiconductor technology. So you’re taking Michael and the Lumentum team’s proprietary technology, we’re actually going to create solutions around those that are highly sticky. And the reason that they’re sold out is because the demand is exploding. And we don’t see that changing anytime soon. But through cycle, the differentiated and proprietary nature of these products is going to be lasting, whether we’re in a supply shortage or not. And I have a lot of experience in these types of technologies. At my prior company, I worked for 22 years at Maxim, where we had unique BiCMOS technologies, bipolar technologies, silicon germanium. We fabbed them ourselves. This is very unique. This isn’t like just laying down regular CMOS. These are very esoteric, black magic process technologies with special people to develop them. So highly proprietary. And by the way, we also see the... we were able to cross-check their demand as well versus ours because we’re all on the same systems, and it gave us a lot of comfort in the growth rate of Lumentum because we’re both in the same sets of designs. Now we can go faster together and enable our customers in a much more meaningful way on time to market. David Faber: Okay, well, you say not changing anytime soon and, you know, soon could be... I mean, again, I’m curious as to why you believe that. And then beyond that, can you comment at all on the regulatory front? Not here in the U.S. as much, but concern around China and China approval. It is notable that you don’t see closing this deal until the first half of 2028. Matt Murphy: Sure. Yeah, yeah. On the first one, again, you know, the transition with what we call the copper wall, going from scale-out optics to scale-up optics... By the way, five years ago, as Mike mentioned, we acquired a company called Inphi, which was doing about 700 million in annualized revenue. It was at the dawn of the scale-out optical transition. That business looked expensive when we bought it, quite frankly. And now that business has grown something like a factor of ten in revenues over a five-year period, that’s on the scale-out side. So something that looked expensive back then turned out to be one of the most compelling acquisitions we’ve ever done. When scale-up hits the end demand, David, is what’s going to drive this, because all these systems need to come together for optical connectivity on scale-up and inside the rack. That’s going to continue agnostic of any kind of supply-demand perturbations. On the regulatory front, we’re very well versed with how to go through that process. The first big acquisition that we did was announced in 2017. It was a company called Cavium. We went through regulatory at that time and closed it in about 7 or 8 months. And that was during the beginning of a lot of the rhetoric and kind of concerns about U.S.-China and how to get things approved. Got that done very efficiently. We went and got Inphi done in about five months, which was very efficient as well. So we have a good sense of what it’s going to take to get the deal closed. The businesses are highly complementary and there is no overlap between the two. So from a substantive basis, it’s a very clean deal. We just need to work efficiently, as we always do, and constructively with the global regulators and get it closed. We did allow, as we normally do—and you see this in semi deals, David—you want to allow for more ample time to get it all done. But we’re going to be off to the races very soon here. David Faber: But China can be a black box Matt, that China can be a bit of a black box. You’re confident though. Matt Murphy: Yes. Very confident. David Faber: Okay. Jim Cramer: Now I do want to ask Michael. We see the stock down and I think people at home might say, wait a second. Are we going to be losers long term? I understand Matt’s record for all the deals he’s done, but are you confident that this creates value for your shareholders from this level? Michael Hurlston: Yeah. Jim, look, we’re not playing for the share price today, for tomorrow, for next week. We’re playing for the long term. And as Matt correctly said, the opportunity I think in front of both companies is enormous. We think there’s incredible revenue synergies, obviously cost synergies that we think we can extract. So I’m not overly concerned about how the stock price is reacting today or has in the last couple of days since this rumor started emerging. I feel really, really good about where Matt can take the combination over the next three, four, or five years. Jim Cramer: Now, you were on our show last when you got a big stake from Nvidia. Nvidia $2 billion convertible preferred. Matt, you’ve taken money from Nvidia too. How does Jensen... what has he said about this deal? And do you think this is the beginning of the end of Jensen doing so-called circular deals? Because to me what he’s done is create an ecosystem that would not exist and may not even have led to such an extraordinary deal. What’s Jensen’s role in this? Matt Murphy: Yeah. Well, what I’d say is within a, I don’t know, a short period of time, both Michael and I struck very different deals, by the way, with Nvidia in terms of what we can go do together, both highly strategic in nature. Michael’s was more on the supply side. Ours was more on the strategic, you know, cooperation on a bunch of new technologies, but both similar levels of investment. And the way that we’re looking at this and positioned to the Nvidia team, which has been, you know, extremely well received, is this is a double down on supporting Nvidia, who is the ecosystem leader in this area. We’re very committed to that partnership. And I think having two of the key suppliers come together that support that ecosystem is going to be a big benefit for them and the Nvidia ecosystem around it. So I think it’s going to be a great deal for them. David Faber: Michael, finally, I mean, Jim referenced this as well. Obviously, the stock is not, you know, even near where it was a week ago and the rumors first came out. You talk a lot in the releases, I think about extreme co-design, a lot of extreme. I’m just curious, can you give our viewers a sense right now of a concrete product that a hyperscaler would get, that they don’t have today that would be reflective of what you’re talking about? Michael Hurlston: Yeah. We talked about quite a bit on the call that the puck is being moved toward what we call co-packaged or near-packaged optics. And in co-packaged and near-packaged optics, you have a lot of Matt’s electronic technology, semiconductor technology, and you have a lot of our optical technology. And what we expect to do is work together with the Marvell team to bring a complete solution where we’re not selling just our bits, our optical bits, and the Marvell team is not just selling their semiconductor bits. We’re going to actually go to market with a complete solution. We think that enables faster time to market, more differentiation by understanding each other’s side of the fence. And we think that that’s going to really propel our customers and propel the customer opportunity forward. So a lot going on on that front. I think that’s really the value of the deal is this extreme co-design and bringing together both sides of the fence in that fashion. Jim Cramer: Well, I want to thank both of you. Matt Murphy from Marvell. Congratulations. And congratulations to Michael Hurlston from Lumentum. Appreciate you coming on CNBC first. Matt Murphy: Thanks, Jim. Michael Hurlston: Thanks, Jim. Jim Cramer: Good to see you. See you soon. David Faber: Thank you guys.
