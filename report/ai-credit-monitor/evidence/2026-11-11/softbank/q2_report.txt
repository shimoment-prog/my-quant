projects. As a result of the  realignment and SB7’s obligations to  its suppliers, the Company  recognized a loss of 
¥597,248 million ($3.73 billion) in “Other gain (loss)” in the condensed interim consolidated statement of profit or 
loss for the  six-month period ended  September 30, 2026. This loss comprised  ¥457,030 million  ($2.85 billion) 
related to non-refundable advance payments made to secure manufacturing capacity and purchase equipment, and 
¥140,218 million ($0.88 billion) related to contract modifications and settlements.
SB7 became entitled to receive rebates of up to $2.1 billion  over the next 10 years  on future equipment  purchases, 
subject to certain conditions.  As the amount of rebates  could not be  determined as of September  30, 2026, the 
Company did not recognize  the rebate  rights as an asset  in the condensed interim  consolidated  statement  of 
financial position.

measured at fair value  every quarter, with changes in fair  value recorded as gains and losses on investments in  the 
consolidated statement of profit or loss.
Investment in listed stocks, corporate bonds and other instruments by the asset management subsidiary

income from bond investments. Since  inception, cumulative investment loss has totaled  ¥703.4 billion.*1 As of the 
second quarter-end, its investment  balance totaled ¥1,270.1 billion,  including ¥1,079.9 billion  in corporate  bonds.

OVERVIEW
In this segment, SoftBank Corp. and  its subsidiaries primarily  provide mobile  services and sell  mobile devices  in 
Japan, as well  as offer broadband services, solution  services and cloud  and AI services for enterprise customers, 
advertising and commerce-related services, and payment and financial services. For individual customers, reflecting 
the growing importance  of the  group’s ecosystem integrating  loyalty  points, payments, financial  services,  e-
commerce and other offerings, the segment is working to expand customer referrals across services and the group’s 
ecosystem. It does so  by maintaining  telecommunications services as a stable earnings  base while leveraging  its 
telecommunications customer base and a broad range of customer  touchpoints, including Yahoo! JAP AN, LINE and 
PayPay. Under the growth strategy “Activate AI for Society,” the segment has positioned the Enterprise business as 
a key pillar supporting this growth. Building on its telecommunications customer base, it is expanding the provision 
of advanced digital  transformation  (DX) solutions and cloud and AI services.  It is also building out  AI 
infrastructure, including AI computing  platforms and AI data  centers, while developing  revenue streams  from this 
infrastructure.
FINANCIAL RESULTS
Net sales increased ¥397,687 million (11.7%) year on year to ¥3,798,146 million, reflecting revenue growth across 
all businesses. Segment income increased ¥76,124 million (12.9%) year on year to ¥668,017 million.
The increase in segment income was attributable to higher profit in the Financial and Enterprise businesses, as well 
as investment gains. In the  Financial business, profit  increased due to  higher payment  fee income resulting  from 
growth in gross merchandise value of QR code  payments and credit card services, as well  as an increase in interest 
income driven  by growth in credit card  finance receivables,  including  revolving credit  and installment  payment 
balances, and growth in bank loan  balances. In the  Enterprise business, profit increased  significantly year on year 
due to higher cloud and AI revenue, particularly from AI computing platforms.

OVERVIEW
In this segment, semiconductor-related subsidiaries such as Arm, Ampere and  Graphcore are working together  to 
enhance the group’s semiconductor business toward the realization of Artificial Superintelligence (ASI).
Arm delivers  industry-leading  compute platforms  that combine  exceptional  performance  with superior energy 
efficiency, with its technologies  deployed across a broad spectrum  of products, including  smartphones, data-center 
servers, IoT devices  and automotive  systems. Arm primarily  generates  revenue from licensing  individual 
semiconductor IP and Compute Subsystems (CSS), as well as Arm Total Access (ATA), which provides access to a 
broad portfolio  of Arm IP  over a  specified period,  and from royalties  earned when customers ship chips 
incorporating Arm technology. License revenue is recognized  either at a point in  time or over time,  depending on 
the terms of each  agreement,  while royalty  revenue is recognized  as customers ship chips incorporating  Arm 
technology. In March 2026, Arm announced  Arm  AGI CPU, its first  Arm-designed silicon chip.  Designed 
specifically as a data  center CPU for general-purpose cloud and agentic  AI workloads, this product extends Arm’s 
compute platform into the  realm of proprietary  silicon chips.  This development  is expected  to further accelerate 
innovation across Arm’s entire  ecosystem. Ampere  specializes  in designing high-performance,  energy-efficient 
CPUs for AI-driven workloads, built  on the Arm compute  platform. Graphcore  has a proven track record  in the 
design and development of semiconductor chips specialized for AI.
These semiconductor-related subsidiaries will continue to invest in R&D to build the future of AI computing, while 
advancing the development of new technologies,  including new compute platforms delivered as semiconductor IP, 
chips and related technologies, which will enable their customers to develop next-generation products.

FINANCIAL RESULTS
Net sales
Net sales in this section are presented in U.S. dollars as the segment’s revenue is primarily denominated in U.S. 
dollars.
Net sales increased $446 million  (23.7%) year on year  to $2,330 million, primarily driven  by higher revenue at 
Arm. This was mainly  attributable  to increases  in both Arm’s license  and other revenue  and royalty revenue. 
License revenue increased as a result of high-value, long-term licensing agreements with multiple major technology 
companies, including six CSS and seven ATA agreements. Royalty revenue  increased across all  three business 
units: Cloud AI (data centers  and networks), Edge AI (smartphones, PCs and IoT devices)  and Physical AI 
(autonomous vehicles and robotics).
In Cloud AI, the expanded adoption of Arm-based server chips, including AWS Graviton, Google Axion, Microsoft 
Cobalt, and NVIDIA  Grace and  Vera, contributed to  higher revenue. In Edge  AI,  the  positive impact  of higher 
royalty rates per  chip, reflecting  wider adoption of Armv9 technology  and CSS, more than  offset lower sales of 
smartphones and other consumer electronics  products amid higher memory  prices. In Physical AI, higher  adoption 
of Arm-based chips  for in-vehicle  infotainment  (IVI) and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS),  together 
with the initial adoption of Armv9-based chips subject to higher royalty rates, contributed to revenue growth.
Segment income
Segment income deteriorated ¥275,095 million year  on year, resulting in a segment loss of ¥322,050 million.  This 
was primarily attributable to an increase of ¥335,170 million  in selling,  general and  administrative expenses. The 
increase reflected higher research and development expenses at Arm and Graphcore, particularly higher share-based 
compensation expenses, as they hired additional engineers to develop next-generation technologies that will support 
future growth. The high level  of research and  development  expenses at Ampere, which became  a subsidiary  in 
November 2025, also contributed to the deterioration in segment income.
Arm, Ampere and Graphcore  use share-based compensation  as a component  of employee incentive  compensation. 
Arm mainly  grants restricted  share units (RSUs), while Ampere and  Graphcore grant phantom  shares linked to 
Arm’s share price.

(3) Management fees and performance fees
Terms and conditions  of management  fees, performance-linked  management  fees, and  performance fees, 
included in segment income from the SoftBank Vision Funds business, are as follows.
a. Management fees and performance fees in SVF1
Management fees to SBIA from SVF1  are, in accordance  with the limited  partnership agreement, calculated 
by multiplying 1% per annum  by Equity contributions  used to fund investments  and paid to SBIA by SVF1 
quarterly. A clawback provision is attached to the management fees received, which is triggered under certain 
conditions based on future investment performance.
Same as the performance-based  distributions,  the amount  of the performance  fees to SBIA from SVF1  is 
calculated using the allocation  method as specified in  the limited  partnership agreement.  SBIA is entitled  to 
receive the performance fees when SVF1 receives cash through disposition, dividend, and monetization of an
