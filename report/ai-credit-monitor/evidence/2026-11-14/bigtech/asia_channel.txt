We may make money when you click on links to our partners. Learn More Alphabet’s AI ambitions are getting expensive, but investors still appear eager to help foot the bill. Google’s parent company raised nearly $3.8 billion through its first Hong Kong and Singapore dollar bond offerings, according to term sheets reviewed by Reuters . The seven-part sale attracted more than $7 billion in orders, highlighting strong demand for the company’s debt. The fundraising adds another chapter to Alphabet’s global borrowing push as it invests heavily in data centers and AI infrastructure. For Google Cloud partners, the bigger question is how that financial firepower could translate into new enterprise opportunities. Here’s how the tranches broke down: Currency Maturity Yield HKD 2-year 4.35% HKD 5-year 4.70% HKD 10-year 5.20% SGD 2-year 2.50% SGD 5-year 3.00% SGD 10-year 3.40% SGD 30-year 3.80% Every tranche priced tighter than initial guidance, meaning investors accepted lower returns than Alphabet originally offered. The bonds settle Nov. 19 and carry expected ratings of Aa2 from Moody’s and AA+ from S&P. Why Alphabet is borrowing like a startup Advertisement This isn’t about covering payroll. Alphabet has committed up to $205 billion in capital expenditures for 2026, largely for data centers and AI chips, per The Next Web . After generating $73.3 billion in reported free cash flow in 2025 (or $38.1 billion on a levered basis), Alphabet posted its first negative quarterly free cash flow since its 2004 IPO in Q2 2026 as AI infrastructure spending surged. Its global bond spree reflects that reality. Alphabet raised €6 billion ($6.7 billion) in euro bonds just last week, sold $25 billion in US dollar bonds in August, and added A$5.5 billion ($3.8 billion) in Australian dollar bonds the same month—signaling to investors that tapping the US debt market twice a year is becoming a regular habit. Alphabet’s debt playbook is a portfolio strategy Here’s what separates this move from standard corporate fundraising: Alphabet is treating global bond markets like a diversified investment portfolio, except it’s the one being invested in. Each new currency represents a fresh pool of lenders who haven’t yet loaded up on AI-related debt . Hong Kong and Singapore savers are simply the latest entrants. The strategy reduces dependence on any single market and creates pricing benchmarks across regions. Advertisement Yet a key risk remains: with Big Tech’s aggregate AI debt expanding past $350 billion this summer, IMF estimates reveal that hyperscalers represent roughly 10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, a massive jump from less than 1% in 2024. As Alphabet continues to expand its borrowing, it pushes the boundaries of market demand to see how far investor appetite truly extends. What this means for channel partners For IT solution providers and resellers, Alphabet’s borrowing capacity signals continued investment in cloud infrastructure and AI services that partners can sell against. Google Cloud’s expansion depends on this capital flow. The strong Asian demand also suggests international investors remain confident in Alphabet’s creditworthiness despite market jitters about AI spending . That’s a positive signal for partners building practices around Google’s enterprise offerings. However, the bond sale does not guarantee new partner incentives, lower cloud prices, or immediate improvements in service availability.
