HSBC sold US$300mil troubled data centre loan at discount

TheStar Tue, Nov 17, 2026 03:33pm - 17 hours View Original


HSBC Holdings Plc offloaded a roughly US$300mil piece of a loan to build a troubled New Mexico data center project at a steep discount, underscoring deepening anxiety surrounding the AI boom.

The bank sold the slice of an US$18bil financing package - which funds construction of the campus in Doña Ana County tied to Oracle Corp. - at prices in the mid-80s, according to people familiar with the matter. The sale took place several weeks ago, shortly after Oracle sent a notice to the project’s developer citing force majeure, the people said, asking not to be identified discussing private information.

HSBC’s move comes as lenders weigh their exposure to AI debt alongside renewed regulatory pushback following a Democratic sweep. Jensen Huang’s abrupt departure from Nvidia Corp., a linchpin of AI’s rapid expansion, has also triggered concerns about the pace of AI growth.

The massive venture in southern New Mexico known as Project Jupiter has been beset by regulatory hurdles and opposition. Oracle sent the force majeure notice to the developer, a unit of Blue Owl Capital Inc., in an attempt to put off payments should the venture get derailed, Bloomberg reported in late September.

Summary Overview
• HSBC sold a US$300 million portion of an US$18 billion financing package for the troubled Project Jupiter data center in New Mexico at a steep discount, with prices in the mid-80s, signaling deepening anxiety over the sustainability of the AI infrastructure boom.

• The sale occurred shortly after Oracle Corp. issued a force majeure notice to the developer, a Blue Owl Capital unit, to delay payments amid significant regulatory hurdles, including delays in acquiring essential air and pipeline permits for the 2.45-gigawatt campus.

• This move by HSBC reflects a broader trend where lenders are reassessing their exposure to AI-related debt, driven by renewed regulatory scrutiny following a Democratic sweep and market concerns triggered by Jensen Huang's abrupt departure from Nvidia Corp.

• The transaction is notable because banks holding construction project financing loans typically retain them on their balance sheets until completion, meaning early exits often require selling at a discount that exceeds the upfront fees received, as seen in this syndicated loan involving approximately 20 banks.
